Every simulation this model has produced, replayed day by day across eleven seasons. Each as-of date re-estimates every club's scoring rates from its last 19 matches at that venue, then simulates the rest of the season to a full table — giving a title probability, a relegation probability and a complete finishing-position distribution for all twenty clubs on every matchday.

How many points keep you up?

This has no league-wide answer, and averaging one out would be misleading. The cut fell on 36 points in 2019 and on 43 in 2017 — whether a total is safe depends entirely on how tightly bunched the bottom of that table is. And a finished season has no probabilities left in it: a club on 44 points either went down or it did not.

So this is one season, from one date, conditioned on the standings at that moment and the fixtures still to play. Move the date slider back to watch a live season's answer sharpen as the season closes.

Against another forecaster

Beating a base rate is a low bar. chancedegol.com.br publishes, for every played match, the probabilities it gave beforehand — so both models can be scored on identical matches. Their probabilities sum to 1.000, so this is model against model, with no bookmaker margin to strip out.

RPS — Ranked Probability Score. The standard metric for 1X2 forecasts. Home, draw and away are ordered: a draw sits between a home win and an away win. RPS works on cumulative probabilities, so predicting a home win costs less when the match is drawn than when the away side wins. Brier treats the three as unrelated labels and charges the same either way. Lower is better; 0 is perfect.

Is the model calibrated?

A forecast of 40% should come true about 40% of the time. This pools every relegation forecast across all eleven seasons and asks whether it does.

The trap that made us retract an earlier finding. A club sitting in the same probability band for two months contributes sixty forecasts and exactly one outcome. Counting forecasts as the sample size makes a handful of clubs look like a large, decisive dataset — and it made a "relegation overconfidence" result look real when it was noise.

So the observed rate below is computed per club, one club one outcome, and each point carries a 95% interval reflecting how few clubs that really is.

What the archive is made of

Iteration counts are not uniform, and not even uniform within a season. Nine seasons ran a fixed 20,000 per date. 2024 and 2025 were built up incrementally during those seasons at whatever budget was passed at the time, so their counts vary date to date.

A date simulated 10 times cannot express a probability finer than 10%: every number it produces is a multiple of one tenth. The thin dates column counts how many fell under 1,000 iterations — 2025 had 79 such dates before it was rebuilt.

Probabilities come from the batch simulator at the model's default constants: a 19-match per-venue lookback, 4/3/1 recency weights, a 50/50 home-attack / away-defence blend, and the newcomer prior at full strength. All four were swept across ten seasons and none was beaten by more than noise. Club crests come from the bolaondroid project, joined on API-Football team ids; six clubs from the older seasons have no crest and fall back to a coloured mark.
